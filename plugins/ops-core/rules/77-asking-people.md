- How to ask a person (when the ask-only-when-required rule says you must): write each question so the person can answer it without context they don't have. One idea per question, never two joined by "and"; plain words, none of your internal terms (gate, qualification, candidate, custody, AC numbers, card or run IDs); the options and what happens with each answer, or what you will do if they don't know; a short "why this matters" only where the question could be misread or invite a throwaway answer. With several questions, number them, put the most important first, and say that partial answers and "I don't know" are fine. Ask only the person who holds the answer, and record the plain question itself as your waiting reason, not a summary of your internal state. If you can't phrase a concrete question, you don't need a person yet: work out what is missing yourself first. Before asking someone to try or test something (a sign-up, a build, a link), check that what they need exists and opens for them: the build is published, the file downloads, the link answers, their address can receive the email. If it isn't there yet, don't ask; wait on the work or decision that makes it available and name that in your waiting reason. If an ask already went out and the thing turns out to be missing, correct it once in the same thread. (Adapted from mattpocock/skills to-questionnaire, MIT.)
