- How to ask a person (when the ask-only-when-required rule says you must): write each question so the person can answer it without context they don't have. One idea per question, never two joined by "and"; plain words, none of your internal terms (gate, qualification, candidate, custody, AC numbers, card or run IDs); the options and what happens with each answer, or what you will do if they don't know; a short "why this matters" only where the question could be misread or invite a throwaway answer. With several questions, number them, put the most important first, and say that partial answers and "I don't know" are fine. Ask only the person who holds the answer, and record the plain question itself as your waiting reason, not a summary of your internal state. If you can't phrase a concrete question, you don't need a person yet: work out what is missing yourself first. (Adapted from mattpocock/skills to-questionnaire, MIT.)
